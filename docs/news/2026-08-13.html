Why it matters: As AI interconnects move from 800G toward 1.6T, indium phosphide (InP) supply constraints and crystal-growth challenges are prompting a shift to GaAs for short-reach optics — a material-level bottleneck with direct implications for transceiver supply and substrate vendors.

First-order effect: InP scarcity constrains high-speed transceiver output at 1.6T; GaAs substrate demand (AXTI) rises for short-reach links, reshaping component sourcing.

Second-order effect: Vendors scaling InP manufacturing (COHR explicitly) gain differentiation; the parallel CPO-vs-pluggable debate signals 409.6T switches may eventually force co-packaged optics, though pluggables remain mainstream near-term.

Bullish implication: COHR (InP scaling), AXTI (GaAs substrates), and transceiver leaders (LITE, FN) benefit from supply-tightness and material transitions.

Bearish risk: Material substitution and CPO timelines are multi-year; near-term this is a technical demand signal, not a revenue inflection. DigiTimes sourcing carries moderate confidence.
