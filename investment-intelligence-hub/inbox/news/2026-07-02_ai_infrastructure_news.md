## Infrastructure Implication

### Most Bullish Signal
The strongest signal is the Bloomberg report that **Meta is building a cloud business to sell excess AI compute**, paired with its ~1.6 GW Crusoe compute lock-in. If accurate, it confirms a new well-capitalized hyperscale-scale buyer committing to a long compute runway and intensifying the multi-cloud arms race against AWS, Azure, and Google. That translates into sustained downstream demand across GPUs (NVDA), networking (ANET/AVGO), optical interconnect (CRDO/COHR/LITE/FN), and power infrastructure — the full AI infrastructure stack.

Nvidia topping data center Ethernet switch revenue (IDC Q1 2026) is structurally important but rests on single-quarter data that may reflect GPU-bundled shipment timing rather than durable share capture; monitor whether Arista and Broadcom respond and whether the trend persists into subsequent quarters. Power-financing headlines (Brookfield/Bloom, National Grid/Joulent) reinforce the power-as-bottleneck thesis but involve multi-year deployment timelines with limited immediate compute-supplier impact.

### Weakest Signal
The $752B-by-2034 market-sizing report and China power-chip price-hike article are directionally supportive but low in immediate actionability — the former is a long-horizon projection, and the latter reflects region-specific pricing driven partly by raw-material inflation rather than pure demand. The Supermicro export-control item is meaningful for SMCI's regulatory overhang but the underlying demand read is already well established.
